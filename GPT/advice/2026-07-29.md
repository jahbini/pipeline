**Recommendation**: add two keys to the `llm:` block of the
`run_lora_train_ite` step in `config/train_lora.yaml`:

    llm:
      batchSize: 1
      iters: 300
      maxSeqLength: 640
      learningRate: 0.00001
      loraRank: 8
      loraAlpha: 16
      loraDropout: 0.05
      loraLayers: 8
      stepsPerReport: 10
      stepsPerEval: 50        # was default 200 → one reading; 50 gives six
      saveEvery: 100
      cacheLimitMB: 2048      # was default 512; ~4GB model, cache had no headroom

Nothing else touches. Both keys are already consumed by
`mlx/lora/train.coffee` (`opts.cacheLimitMB ? 512` at line 281;
`opts.stepsPerEval ? 200` at line 211).

**Why**:

- `cacheLimitMB`: `mx.tidy` around forward+backward+update makes the
  per-step 512 MB rail unnecessary; raising it lets the allocator reuse
  instead of round-tripping to the OS. Cheap upside, easily reverted.
- `stepsPerEval`: at 300 iters and the default 200, one eval isn't a
  trend. Six readings tell you whether to climb the iters ladder.
  Upstairs Claude's math: 300 uniform-with-replacement draws over ~900
  rows covers ~28% unique (`1−(1−1/900)^300 ≈ 0.283`), so weak voice may
  be a coverage problem, not a learning-rate one. `valBatches` defaults
  to 25; six evals add ~150 extra batches to the run — one-time cost for
  the signal.

**How to verify**: one run of the `train_lora` recipe, then read
`out/lora_train.txt`:

1. `mem peak=..GB` line every 10 steps — accept the change if peak stays
   **flat from step 30 to step 300**. A higher-but-flat peak is fine; a
   lower-but-monotonically-rising peak means `tidy` isn't covering
   something and the 512 rail was load-bearing (revert `cacheLimitMB`).
2. Six `valid_loss=` lines instead of one. If loss is still falling at
   step 300 → next run set `iters: 600`, then 900, judging on diary
   entries (`valid_loss` tells you whether to bother; diary reading is
   the actual test). If it has turned up → 300 is right, don't chase.

Do NOT change `learningRate`, `iters`, or `batchSize` in this patch —
those are separate experiments once the eval trend is legible.
